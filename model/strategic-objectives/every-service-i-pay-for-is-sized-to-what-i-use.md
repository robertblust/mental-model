# Every service I pay for is sized to what I use

> No cloud service costs more in a month than I decided it may, and no subscription sits on a tier my use does not need.

## What it makes true

Two ways of paying for a service fail in opposite directions. A metered service, billed for what it used, fails by running past what I meant to spend, and I learn of it from the bill or from the service stopping. A subscription, billed the same whatever it carries, fails by paying for room I never use. When this holds, each metered service has a cap written down as a decision and no month goes past it except under a decided exception, and each subscription's tier is one my use in the month actually needed.

It fails when a month's spend on a metered service passes its cap with no decision allowing it, and when a subscription stays on its tier through months in which a smaller one would have carried the same work.

What falls outside: whether the money is well spent against what it produced, which the cost page of the Deciding well talk answers; my own hours, which no plan or cap sizes; and any service that costs nothing.
