# CompanyGraph — an introduction

> A narrated talk that puts CompanyGraph up for scrutiny: a meta-model for operating a company, the structure its knowledge takes so people and AI can both rely on it, open source and not finished.

## Achievements

### Context

- Gave it as the talk on companygraph.io, in August 2026: an idea presented for pushback rather than applause, and then four questions.

### Ways of working

- Fixed the tone and the limits in the speaker notes before the deck was built: what exists is stated as intent and never as status, the roadmap says which parts exist rather than the slide, no count is recited, and the provenance is one sentence, built inside a company with a payroll and then needed in the same shape by a company of one, with no names.

### Sharing

- Opened on the tax every company pays: its knowledge is written down somewhere, no two people would look in the same place, nobody knows which version is true today, and so people ask a person instead of reading.
- Set CompanyGraph against that: one structured knowledge base, the facts as Markdown structured by a meta-model and versioned like code, a projection of how the company runs rather than documentation that gathers dust, where every box is a page a person reads, its title is its name and anything with parts of its own keeps them with it.
- Named the difference from a wiki: pages refer to each other by the name of the thing and not by a link, so a rule with nobody accountable, a role nobody holds or a metric without a target shows up at once and nothing is quietly lost; and the gate from a proposal to work that gets built is data, who signs it off, what has to be true and which rule sits behind it, so that nothing gets built before the sign-off is checkable rather than culture.
- Stated the thesis the model ships under: the schemas are Markdown themselves, an agent reads the same table a checker reads and there is no second format kept for the program, so with the right meta-model the prose is the schema; and said what it does for an AI without overclaiming, that it shrinks what the AI has to guess and holds it inside the rules a person wrote.
- Closed on four questions where a no is worth more than a polite yes: where a company does not fit the shape, whether Markdown checked by agents is trusted or a schema language is needed first, whether an instance would be set up with a tool or by copying a folder of schemas, and whether anyone would pay for help building one, time and material and nothing hosted.
- Published it as a self-contained bilingual deck with a transport bar, narration recorded from the speaker notes in each language and a PDF of each.
