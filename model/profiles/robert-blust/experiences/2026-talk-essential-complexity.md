# Essential Complexity

> A narrated talk on describing a problem in its essential complexity, no more and no less: the standard has held for fifteen years, and what changed is the cost of meeting it.

## Achievements

### Context

- Gave it as a talk on blust.ch, in August 2026, in German first and then in English: one standard for a model, held across four fields of a career, software development, methodology, IT architecture and business architecture, and what AI changed about reaching it.

### Ways of working

- Held the resolution back on purpose: the opener sets the tension, one continuous trace from business architecture down to implementation that was long expensive and hard to reach, and the pivot and the punchline come only later, so the room meets the standard before it hears that the path to it is now open, and the punchline says a model gives AI the right context and shrinks the space where it has to guess, without the claim that hallucinations disappear.

### Sharing

- Set the standard first: a good model captures a problem in its essential complexity, no more and no less, the measure held for every model over about fifteen years.
- Traced why the standard was expensive to meet, UML, SysML or a DSL of one's own, done for real as canonical data models at UBS and custom DSLs at 3AP and LIKE MAGIC, and why it no longer is: AI reads natural language, so with the right meta-model the facts are written as Markdown at the same precision with far less friction.
- Showed it on LIKE MAGIC's Finance domain, where I led the IT architecture, the business architecture and the production platform: every term defined in one sentence, Folio as the financial account of a reservation attached to a Debitor, collecting order items and payments and ending in an invoice; requirements written only in the glossary's terms, the implementation checked against the glossary and the architecture decisions, and a new term landing in the glossary before anything merges, which is the check an AI can now run.
- Placed it in the industry: agile dropped upfront modeling as too slow and too expensive, and with AI making a specification cheap to capture the pendulum swings back to spec-driven development, which is how I still build, close to the code and with the model in mind.
- Published it as a self-contained bilingual deck with hand-drawn journey panels as a side motif, a transport bar, narration recorded from the speaker notes in each language and a PDF of each.
