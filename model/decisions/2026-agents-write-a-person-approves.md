# Agents write the model, and I approve every change to it

> The pages of my model are written by agents, on my request or in a workflow that runs on its own, and no change enters the model until I have approved it; the checks prove a change has the right shape, and only I say it is true.

## The question

Who writes my model, and who lets a change into it. Agents already draft and implement under the seats my processes name, and CompanyGraph decided on September 29, 2026 that a company's model is written this way. It had to be decided for my model then too, because the model is what a client or an employer reads about me, and a claim that entered unread would be read as mine.

## Alternatives

| Option | Why not |
| --- | --- |
| I write the pages myself | The format costs me time the agents spend without effort, and that time is better spent on whether a page is true. |
| A change from a workflow that runs on its own merges once the checks pass | A check proves a page has the right shape, not that it is true, and the model would then claim things about me that I never read. |

## Why

The one thing an agent cannot supply about me is whether a claim is true, and it is the one thing a decider reading my model relies on. So the agents write and I approve: my time goes to the judgment, not the typing.

## Consequences

Every change reaches the model through my approval, whether I asked for it or a workflow made it, and nothing merges on the checks alone. Answers on the chat change nothing and still go out unread. I may still edit a page by hand. The call stays right for as long as I actually read each change before approving it; an approval given without reading leaves a claim about me that nobody checked.
